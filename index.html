Documentation eats into time with patients

Every ward round, clinic, and discharge ends with the same task: turning what happened into a letter. The facts are usually already written down somewhere — in the notes, in bullet points scrawled between patients — but shaping them into a properly structured letter still takes real time, out of a day that's already full.

Clinidraft is an attempt to close that gap. Give it the bullet points, and it produces a structured first draft in the right format, so a doctor's time goes into checking and refining rather than starting from a blank page.

How it works

Three steps, and a doctor stays in control at every one of them.

01

Jot the findings

Write the clinical findings as bullet points, the way you'd already note them during a ward round or clinic.

02

Clinidraft drafts the letter

It structures those points into the right format for a discharge summary, referral, or clinic letter — flagging anything it wasn't told, rather than guessing.

03

The doctor reviews and signs

Every draft is checked, edited, and approved by a doctor before it goes anywhere near a patient record. Clinidraft drafts; it doesn't decide.

Try it

This runs for real. Pick a letter type, enter a few fictional findings, and generate a draft.

Runs a small AI model inside your browser, so nothing you type leaves your device. The first time, it downloads roughly 1 GB, so use Wi-Fi. It needs a recent browser with WebGPU (current Chrome or Edge, or Safari on recent iPads and Macs). Small models make more mistakes than Claude, so check every line.

Stays in your browser and is sent straight to Anthropic to generate this one draft. It isn't saved anywhere and is forgotten when you close or refresh the page. Get a key at console.anthropic.com.

What this is — and isn't

Clinical documentation is a reasonable place for AI to help, but only within clear limits. These are the ones Clinidraft works inside of.

A drafting aid, not a decision-maker

Every draft must be reviewed, edited, and approved by a qualified clinician before it's used for a real patient. Clinidraft never sends, files, or submits anything on its own.

No real patient data

This demo isn't connected to any clinical system. It should only ever be given fictional or fully anonymised details — nothing that could identify a real person.

It says what it doesn't know

In both AI modes, the model is instructed to mark missing information explicitly — for example [not documented] — rather than invent findings. It's still a prototype built on a general-purpose model, not a clinically validated product.

AI mode uses your own key

Clinidraft has no server of its own, so this site stores and logs nothing. On-device AI keeps your text in your browser. Claude mode sends your API key and typed findings directly from your browser to Anthropic.
